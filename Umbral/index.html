How an Expedition Comes Together

Every departure follows the same field-tested arc, from booking to the moment you're standing in the shadow itself.

Pick an expedition and hold one of 24 seats with a fully refundable deposit. You'll get a packing list built for the specific site — altitude, temperature swings, and all.

Three weeks out, our staff astronomer runs a live video briefing on the mechanics of the event, what to expect minute by minute, and how to photograph it without missing it with your own eyes.

We build in buffer days before the event for jet lag, altitude, and weather contingency — plus a backup viewing site scouted in advance in case cloud cover moves in.

On event day, telescopes, solar filters, and a guided countdown are provided on site. Afterward, the group reconvenes to trade photos and talk through what everyone just saw.

What a Night Looks Like

Kit on the ground, and someone to point it

Every expedition travels with a working observing setup and the person who knows how to run it. You're not handed a checklist and left to it.

  • Optics provided. Solar filters and a hydrogen-alpha scope for eclipse days; wide-field binoculars and a tracked camera mount for meteor and aurora nights.
  • Guided countdown. A staff astronomer calls the sequence out loud — first contact, shadow bands, diamond ring — so nobody spends totality looking at a screen.
  • Small groups. Capped at 24 travellers, so there's a scope free when you want one and room at the eyepiece.
