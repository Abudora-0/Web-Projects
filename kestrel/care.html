Care & Repairs

Good clothes ask for very little. Wash them less than you think, dry them slowly, and keep them out of the sun. Here's how to look after each fibre we use - and what to do when something wears through.

The short version

  • Wash only when it's actually dirty. Airing overnight fixes most things.
  • Cold water, gentle cycle, garment turned inside out.
  • Never tumble dry knitwear or denim. Dry flat or on the line, out of direct sun.
  • Fold knitwear. Hang shirts and tailoring on shaped hangers.
  • Iron on the reverse, one setting cooler than you'd expect.

Wool & cashmere

Merino Crew, Lambswool Sweater, Shawl-Collar Cardigan, Balmacaan Wool Coat, Pleated Wide Trouser.

Wool is naturally antibacterial and shrugs off odour, so it rarely needs washing. When it does, hand wash in cool water with a little wool detergent, or use a wool cycle at 30°C or below. Don't wring - press the water out, roll in a towel, then reshape and dry flat. Pilling in the first few wears is normal; run a comb or a fabric shaver over it and it settles down.

Store folded with cedar, not hung - the shoulders will stretch. The wool coat is the exception: hang it on a broad wooden hanger and have it dry cleaned once at the end of the season, no more.

Cotton - shirts, tees, chinos

Oxford Shirt, Heavyweight Tee, Twill Chino, Camden Overshirt.

Machine wash warm (up to 40°C), inside out, with like colours. Garment-dyed pieces - the chino, the overshirt - will fade a little over the first few washes, which is intended. Tumble dry low or, better, hang damp and let gravity do the ironing.

Iron the oxford while it's still slightly damp for a crisp collar. The heavyweight tee needs no iron; smooth it flat off the line.

Denim

Selvedge Straight Jean, The Workhorse Denim Jacket.

Raw denim is meant to be worn, not washed. Leave it six months before the first wash if you can - that's how you get fades that map to your own life. When you do wash: turn inside out, cold water, a gentle cycle or a soak in the bath, and line dry. Between washes, air it or spot clean.

Expect the raw indigo to crock (rub off) onto light shoes and pale furniture for the first month. It stops.

Canvas & leather

Canvas Weekender Tote.

The canvas will soften and mark, and the bridle-leather handles will darken with the oils from your hands. That's the point. Spot clean the canvas with a damp cloth; wipe the leather occasionally with a barely-damp cloth and, once a year, a thin coat of neutral leather balm.

Repairs, on us for a year

A dropped hem, a split seam, a button lost, a small moth hole - send any Kestrel piece back within twelve months of buying it and we'll mend it free, return shipping included. After the first year we still do repairs at cost; a re-knit elbow or a full reline is usually a few hundred rupees.

Alterations - taking up a hem, tapering a leg - are free within 30 days of your order. Start a repair.

Stains

  • Fresh - blot, don't rub. Cold water from the back of the fabric.
  • Oil or grease - a little dish soap worked in with a finger, then a normal wash.
  • Red wine, coffee, ink - get it to us or a specialist quickly rather than trying it at home on wool.
  • Deodorant marks - rub the two sides of the fabric together, or a dry sponge.
